About SWAN

The Southern Women Academics Network is a community of women in the Global South committed to making academia a viable career choice.

Our Vision

Making Academia a Viable Career Choice for Women

Who We Are

SWAN works with women in academia across the Global South. We support women pursuing tertiary education from Masters to PhD level, as well as girls in schools and young women in tertiary institutions.

We are driven by a single vision: to make academia a viable, rewarding, and sustainable career choice for women. Through community, mentorship, and practical support, we are building a generation of women scholars who thrive.

Aims & Objectives

What Drives Us

01

Creating an enabling environment to increase women's productivity through facilitating protected writing time for consistent, persistent, and intentional writing towards publication.

02

Equipping women with tools and techniques for enhancing the quality and quantity of output through knowledge exchange and ongoing discourse.

03

Facilitating a strong transdisciplinary network for collaboration, mentoring, supporting, and uplifting each other professionally and socially in relation to work-life balance.

04

Providing spaces for sharing resources and opportunities.

05

Securing funding and other resources to support talented girls and women in resource-poor settings. Equipping girls in schools and young women in higher and tertiary institutions with the knowledge and information necessary to identify academia as a viable career option through career guidance.

Our Founders

Meet the Women Behind SWAN

Dr Nonhlanhla Dube

Co-Founder

Dr Nonhlanhla Dube is a passionate advocate for women's advancement in academia. With extensive experience in research, mentorship, and higher education, she co-founded SWAN to create spaces where women in the Global South can thrive as scholars.

Dr Grascious Maviza

Co-Founder

Dr Grascious Maviza is a dedicated researcher and mentor committed to empowering women through education and collaboration. Her work focuses on building networks that support women's academic productivity and career growth across the Global South.
